Figures are quoted as each source states them. "XHLF" means extra-high-load-factor, the APS rate class for data centers and fabs.

**HB 2756** ("utilities; high load factor customers") was signed on Jun 4, 2026. It requires:
- quarterly ACC reports on new XHLF interconnection requests and completed interconnections;
- a cost-of-service study within 180 days of the effective date;
- XHLF payments that cover the infrastructure built to serve them, with no cross-subsidy of other customers.

In addition, the APS rate case proposes a ">45%" XHLF rate increase.

**How the trend reads:** committed load went from ~3.3 GW of data centers by 2028 (mid-2025) to "4.5-plus GW" of all large load (2026). The uncommitted queue has stayed flat at about 17–20 GW since mid-2025. Conversion depends on new generation and transmission, starting with the Desert Sun-backed subscription tranche of about 1 GW.

## 2. Geographic clusters (APS-served only)

**A. Southwest Valley: Goodyear, Avondale, Tolleson edge, Buckeye east.** This is the largest committed data center cluster.
- Microsoft Goodyear (Runway substation)
- STACK Avondale, 225 MW (Diamond substation)
- Stream PHXA: 280 MW critical, behind a 480 MW APS substation
- Vantage AZ1: 176 MW IT
- Compass Goodyear: 212 MW
- Unnamed high-load customers at Broadway, Goodyear and Three Rivers

Stated MW totals roughly 0.9–1.3 GW of IT capacity. Prime Avondale (240 MW) is served by SRP and is excluded.

**B. Northwest Valley: El Mirage, Glendale west, Litchfield Park.**
- Microsoft El Mirage PHX-80 to PHX-83 and Compass El Mirage (108 MW), via the Contrail, Raptor, Sabre and TS39 230 kV lines
- Dromedary high-load customer (Camelback Rd & Cotton Ln)
- Takanock Project Eagle: on-site gas, then an APS substation in 2030

Aligned Glendale's utility is unverified.

**C. North Phoenix (TSMC / Biscuit Flats / Halo Vista).**
- TSMC: more than 1 GW committed before its 2026 $100B expansion. That expansion sits outside the 4.5 GW committed figure.
- Supplier parks and Halo Vista (30M sq ft)
- Served by Avery and Wafer (ex-TS22) 500/230 kV, 2028

**D. Far West: Buckeye, Tonopah, Palo Verde.** This is mostly uncommitted and speculative.
- Tract Buckeye: "up to 1.8GW"
- Hassayampa Ranch (ALC): 1–1.5 GW
- Tonopah 638-acre site
- Copia Harquahala

These are likely part of the ~19–20 GW uncommitted queue.

**E. NW Surprise / Wittmann.** Logistics and industrial load, served by the Outer Circle substation (2027) and the Morgan–Outer Circle line (2030). Project Baccara (700 MW on-site gas) is behind the meter.

**F. Pinal County / Casa Grande.** Manufacturing and industrial load growth served by Bianco (2028). The data center proposals here (Grande Valley 954 ac, Midway) have unconfirmed utilities and on-site gas. La Osa (Eloy) has been reported as not proceeding.

**Excluded as SRP:** the East Valley / Mesa cluster (Google 400 MW, NTT, Novva, EdgeCore, Meta) and QTS Phoenix.

## 4. Mapping to generation-interconnection study areas, and where generation near load is most valuable

| Load cluster | Study area (POIs) | Value of generation near the load |
|---|---|---|
| A. SW Valley (Runway, Diamond, Broadway, Stratus, Independence, Goodyear) | Phoenix Metro | **Highest.** Rudd is the constrained node. Independence and Jojoba–Rudd arrive 2030–31; before then, metro-side resources (especially storage or firm resources at Independence, Runway, Stratus or Broadway) directly relieve Rudd. |
| B. NW Valley (Contrail, Parkway, El Sol, Raptor, Sabre, Dromedary) | Phoenix Metro | High. The 230 kV network is still being built, and Agua Fria–Contrail does not arrive until 2030. |
| C. North Phoenix / TSMC (Wafer, Avery, Raceway, Scatter Wash, Pinnacle Peak) | Phoenix Metro; Delaney–Sun Valley–Redhawk via Morgan | High. Wafer and Morgan–Wafer #2 make Morgan/Raceway-area POIs electrically close to TSMC. |
| D. Far West / Buckeye / Tonopah | Gila Bend (Freedom, Hazen, Panda, Watermelon, Jojoba) and Delaney–Sun Valley–Redhawk (Delaney, Sun Valley, Redhawk, TS30) | Medium-high, conditional. This load is largely uncommitted. Gila Bend generation reaches load through Panda–Freedom and Jojoba–Independence; Desert Sun competes for the same Watermelon/Jojoba paths. |
| E. NW Surprise | Delaney–Sun Valley–Redhawk (Sun Valley, Outer Circle) | Medium. Outer Circle and Morgan–Outer Circle are explicitly meant to enable Delaney/Sun Valley generation. |
| F. Pinal / Casa Grande | South of Phoenix (Bianco, Milligan, Helios, Saguaro, Santa Rosa, TS25) | Medium. Load growth is real but smaller, and Saguaro upgrades add capacity. |

Remote areas (Hoodoo Wash–Yuma, Four Corners–Cholla, NSTS, Northern) are far from load and depend on EHV imports. The Four Corners–Cholla–Pinnacle Peak rebuild (2038) and the Hassayampa–North Gila #2 cut-in (TBD) are the main relief.

**Bottom line:** APS's demand is growing at Rudd and Independence in the Southwest Valley and at Wafer in North Phoenix. Phoenix Metro POIs, followed by Gila Bend POIs east of Jojoba, are the most valuable for serving committed load. Delaney–Sun Valley and Gila Bend gain value only if parts of the ~19–20 GW uncommitted queue (Buckeye, Tonopah) convert through the subscription model.
